## 🎯 2. Business Problem Statement

Beauty salons and spa businesses manage several activities simultaneously, including service delivery, appointment scheduling, pricing, customer bookings, and staff coordination.

Without an effective reporting system, salon managers may find it difficult to understand which services contribute the most to business performance, how booking issues are distributed, and whether staff-related activity requires further investigation.

For example, a salon may experience repeated appointment cancellations, uneven demand across services, or differences in the number of records associated with staff members. These patterns can be difficult to investigate when information is spread across separate tables or spreadsheets.

The lack of consolidated reporting can make it harder to:

- Compare service performance.
- Understand differences in recorded service prices.
- Monitor appointment cancellations and no-shows.
- Investigate scheduling problems.
- Review staff-related activity.
- Identify areas requiring operational improvements.

This project addresses the reporting challenge by organizing relevant salon information into a visual dashboard.

The dashboard allows users to explore the available measures and categories in one place, making it easier to compare values and identify questions for further investigation.

## 📈 11. Key Performance Indicators

The following KPIs could strengthen the dashboard if the required source fields are available.

### 11.1 Total Service Revenue

Measures the total revenue from qualifying completed transactions.

**Formula:**

Total Service Revenue = Sum of qualifying transaction revenue

This calculation requires a valid revenue measure or a reliable way to calculate revenue from completed appointments.

### 11.2 Completed Appointment Count

Measures the number of appointments successfully completed during a selected period.

**Formula:**

Completed Appointments = Count of qualifying completed appointments

Use a unique appointment identifier when available to avoid counting duplicate records as separate appointments.

### 11.3 Cancellation Rate

Measures the proportion of eligible bookings that were canceled.

**Formula:**

Cancellation Rate (%) = Canceled Appointments / Eligible Bookings × 100

The eligible booking population and cancellation definition must be clearly established.

### 11.4 No-Show Rate

Measures the proportion of eligible bookings where customers did not attend.

**Formula:**

No-Show Rate (%) = No-Show Appointments / Eligible Bookings × 100

This KPI requires reliable booking status information.

### 11.5 Average Transaction Value

Measures the average revenue generated per qualifying transaction.

**Formula:**

Average Transaction Value = Total Qualifying Revenue / Number of Qualifying Transactions

The denominator should represent distinct transactions rather than an arbitrary count of rows.

### 11.6 Average Service Price

Measures the average recorded price for a service or selected service category.

**Formula:**

Average Service Price = Sum of Valid Service Prices / Number of Valid Price Records

If service prices repeat across appointments, ensure the calculation answers the intended pricing question.

### 11.7 Rescheduling Rate

Measures the proportion of eligible bookings that were rescheduled.

**Formula:**

Rescheduling Rate (%) = Rescheduled Appointments / Eligible Bookings × 100

The booking status definitions must be consistent, and the same appointment should not be counted multiple times without a defined reason.

### 11.8 Staff Utilization

Measures the proportion of available working time spent delivering services.

**Formula:**

Staff Utilization (%) = Productive Service Hours / Available Working Hours × 100

This KPI requires reliable working-hour and service-duration information.

### 11.9 Service Profit Margin

Measures the percentage of service revenue remaining after the relevant costs have been deducted.

**Formula:**

Service Profit Margin (%) = (Service Revenue − Associated Costs) / Service Revenue × 100

The calculation requires appropriate revenue and cost data.

These are proposed KPIs. They should only be presented as implemented dashboard measures if they have actually been calculated and validated.

## 🚀 12. Business Recommendations

### Recommendation 1: Review Service Pricing and Profitability

Compare service prices with appointment volumes, labor costs, product costs, and other operating expenses.

This can help distinguish services with high prices from services that generate strong overall financial returns.

### Recommendation 2: Investigate Booking Issues

Review cancellations, no-shows, double-bookings, rescheduling, and service issues using clearly defined metrics.

If the data confirms recurring booking problems, investigate potential improvements to scheduling procedures and appointment management.

### Recommendation 3: Improve Appointment Scheduling

If double-bookings or rescheduling are confirmed issues, consider reviewing appointment availability, booking workflows, and communication procedures.

Any proposed changes should be evaluated using subsequent booking data to determine whether they improve outcomes.

### Recommendation 4: Evaluate Staff Workload Appropriately

Use appointment volume, service duration, working hours, and completed bookings to understand staff workload.

Avoid using raw record counts as the sole basis for assessing productivity or employee performance.

### Recommendation 5: Monitor Service Demand

Compare completed appointment volumes and validated revenue by service and time period.

This can help the business investigate changes in demand and plan staff or inventory requirements.

### Recommendation 6: Introduce Operational KPIs

Add validated measures for cancellation rates, no-show rates, completed appointments, and service profitability where the necessary data is available.

These measures would provide a more comprehensive view of business performance.

### Recommendation 7: Strengthen Data Quality

Maintain consistent service labels, clearly defined booking statuses, accurate dates, and reliable numeric measures.

A consistent data structure supports more trustworthy analysis and reporting.
